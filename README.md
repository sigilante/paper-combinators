# Combinator Research Preprints

This repository contains preprints and mechanized proofs related to N. E. Davis' research on combinators, particularly the combinator $S = \lambda x.\lambda y.\lambda z. x z (y z)$ or Smullyan's Starling.  All theorems are mechanized in [`sigilante/lean-ski`](https://github.com/sigilante/lean-ski).

This work was carried out in a centaur fashion, with high-level human direction but the bulk of the mechanization and formalization done by the LLM agent.

## Papers

* [Head Normal Forms of the Combinator *S*](s1-head-normal-forms/main.pdf)
  **It settles Question 10 of "Dance of the Starlings" by proving that their 35-state tree automaton accepts exactly the $S$-terms with a head normal form, so whether such a term ever reaches head form can be decided mechanically.**

  > A closed term built from $S$ alone has a head normal form when it reduces to $S$, $S\,X$ or $S\,X\,Y$. Barendregt, Endrullis, Klop and Waldmann asked whether a 35-state tree automaton they gave accepts exactly these terms ("Dance of the Starlings", Question 10). We prove that it does. The automaton is a homomorphic image of a 38-element model of the $S$-law, so its state is a reduction invariant, and rejection implies the absence of a head normal form. For the converse we read a weight along the spine of a term; a finite certificate of small integers makes the weight, with two tie-breakers, decrease at every head step. The whole proof is mechanized in Lean~4 with no axioms beyond propositional extensionality and quotient soundness, and the finite facts are checked by kernel evaluation.
* [Productive Terms of the Combinator *S*](s2-productive-terms/main.pdf)
  **It answers Question 5 of "Dance of the Starlings": a 39-state tree automaton accepts exactly the productive $S$-terms, those whose Böhm tree has no $\bot$, so this set is regular and productivity is decidable, where before only normalization was known to be decidable.**

  > A closed term built from $S$ alone is productive when its Böhm tree has no $\bot$. Barendregt, Endrullis, Klop and Waldmann asked how to characterize these terms, whether they form a regular set, and whether productivity is decidable ("Dance of the Starlings", Question 5). We give a 39-state bottom-up tree automaton that accepts exactly the productive closed $S$-terms, so the set is regular and productivity is decidable. The automaton is a model of the $S$-law, so its state is a reduction invariant, and acceptance implies productivity by the head-normal-form theorem of a companion paper. The converse reduces to 2,757 pairs of states, which a certificate of 7,244 claims about symbolic head reduction settles. The proof, including the soundness of the certificate checker and its run on the certificate, is mechanized in Lean~4 with no axioms beyond propositional extensionality and quotient soundness, for productivity stated as "the Böhm tree is defined to every depth"; its equivalence with the Böhm-tree statement is a short argument on paper.
* [Infinitary Normal Forms of the Combinator *S*](s3-infinitary-normal-forms/main.pdf)
  **It gives a machine-checked proof of Waldmann's theorem that no reduction of an $S$-term rewrites the root infinitely often, and shows that a term is productive exactly when no node of its infinitary normal form has an infinite left spine, linking the infinite behaviour of $S$-terms to the finite automaton of the previous paper.**

  > Waldmann proved that no reduction of a closed term built from $S$ alone rewrites the root infinitely often. We mechanize his proof in Lean 4, with the case analysis restated over a 38-element model of the $S$-law, so that every side condition becomes a finite table fact. Hereditarily, every infinite reduction from a finite $S$-term converges (mechanized), and each term $t$ has a unique infinitary normal form $t^\infty$, its Berarducci tree (uniqueness is cited; Lean has $t^\infty$ through its finite prefixes). With the head-normal-form automaton of a companion paper, every finite prefix of $t^\infty$ is computable. And $t$ is productive---its Böhm tree has no $\bot$---exactly when no node of $t^\infty$ has an infinite left spine; so a 39-state automaton decides which infinitary normal forms have a node with an infinite left spine.
* [Join Bounds and the Word Problem for the Combinator *S*](s4-word-problem/main.pdf)
  **It shows that Barendregt's 1975 word problem for $S$ (RTA open problem #97) would be solved by a computable bound on the size of common reducts, and it rules out the simplest such bound, an additive one.**

  > Whether convertibility of closed terms built from $S$ alone is decidable has been open since Barendregt asked in 1975. We show that it is decidable if convertible terms always have a common reduct whose size is bounded by a computable function of their sizes. We also show that no bound has the form $\max(|t|,|u|)+c$, by a family of convertible pairs whose common reducts all exceed the larger term by a linear amount. Both results are mechanized in Lean 4.
* [A 373-Element Model Computes the Depth-Two Prefix of an *S*-Term](s5-depth-two-prefixes/main.pdf)
  **It shows that a finite algebra can read two levels of an $S$-term's infinitary normal form, not just whether it has a head normal form, so the terms with a given depth-two prefix form a regular set; it is the first step beyond head normal forms toward finite descriptions of the infinite behaviour of $S$-terms.**

  > Every closed term $t$ built from the combinator $S$ has an infinitary normal form $t^\infty$, whose finite prefixes are computable. We show that the prefix of depth two is computed by a 373-element model of the $S$-law: the fold of $t$ into the model determines it. The 38-element model that decides head normal forms is a homomorphic image of this one. The proof reduces the claim to finitely many conditions on the model's table, and it is mechanized in Lean 4 for the prefix rules of the companion paper on infinitary normal forms.
* [Prefix Regularity for the Combinator *S*](s6-prefix-regularity/main.pdf)
  **It shows that every finite part of an $S$-term's infinitary normal form is computed by a finite algebra, so it is decidable whether any $S$-term has an infinitary normal form beginning with a given tree, and the word problem for $S$ would be decidable if a term's infinitary normal form determined its conversion class.**

  > Every closed term $t$ built from the combinator $S$ has an infinitary normal form $t^\infty$. We show that for every depth $d$ a finite model of the $S$-law computes the depth-$d$ prefix of $t^\infty$: the fold of $t$ into the model determines it. The models are computable from $d$. Consequently, for every finite tree $P$ the terms whose prefix is $P$ form a regular tree language, and it is decidable whether some closed $S$-term has an infinitary normal form beginning with $P$. The proof rests on two bounds on head reduction, one of which uses a finite computation on a 38-element table, and it is mechanized in Lean 4.
* [No Ground Loops for the Combinator $S$, Constructively](s7-no-ground-loops/main.pdf)
  **It gives a constructive, machine-checked proof of Waldmann's theorem that no $S$-term reduces to a term containing itself, so no $S$-term can head-reduce back to itself with extra arguments, emitting them forever; more strongly, no term reduces to a proper superterm of any of its head reducts.**

  > Waldmann proved that no closed term built from the combinator $S$ reduces, in one or more steps, to a term that contains it. We give a constructive proof and mechanize it in Lean 4. We prove termination of a variant of Waldmann's labelled reduction by direct induction on accessibility instead of a recursive path order. The theorem follows by replaying a loop inside its own result. As a consequence, no closed $S$-term head-reduces to itself applied to arguments. More generally, if a term reaches $u$ without contracting an argument, for instance by head steps, then it reduces to no term $C[u]$ with $C\ne[\,]$ a context.
* [No Conversion Fixed Points in Plain Contexts of the Combinator *S*](s8-plain-contexts/main.pdf)
  **It shows that no $S$-term is convertible with a copy of itself placed in a plain context of depth at most two, which gives a new proof of Statman's theorem that $S$ defines no fixed-point combinator and reduces the general case to one stated conjecture, a strong form of the absence of ground loops.**

  > We show that no closed term $p$ of the combinator $S$ is convertible with $E[p]$ when $E\ne[\,]$ is a plain context of depth at most two with a hole. A context is plain if it is built from holes and closed normalizing terms by $E'\mapsto S\,E'$ and $E',E''\mapsto S\,E'\,E''$. The proof rests on three theorems. An infinite right comb has teeth of unbounded size. No nonempty set of terms has these two properties: every member has a head normal form whose items lie in the set or are of bounded size, at least one of them in the set; and the last head step of a member duplicates a large item in two simple ways only. No nonempty set meets only finitely many conversion classes if every member has a head normal form with at least one item and all its items in the set; this settles every plain context without closed parts. The case $E=S\,[\,]$ gives Statman's theorem that $S$ defines no fixed-point combinator.
* [No Closed *S*-Term Is Convertible with Itself Applied to Normalizing Arguments](s9-consumption/main.pdf)
  **It shows, with a machine-checked proof, that an $S$-term never equals itself applied to arguments: for any arguments if the term has a head normal form, and for normalizing arguments in general.**

  > We show that no term $p$ built from the combinator $S$ is convertible with $p\,x_1\cdots x_k$ for $k\ge1$ and normalizing $S$-terms $x_1,\dots,x_k$; if $p$ has a head normal form, the $x_i$ may be arbitrary $S$-terms. In that case a 38-element model that detects head normal forms turns the conversion into a closed walk, and the walk leads to an infinite chain of terms, each of size two less than the last, which cannot exist. Otherwise the head run of $p$ splits off arguments of unbounded size, yet a conversion makes each convertible with some $x_j$, which bounds its size. The results are mechanized in Lean 4.
* [Clock Closure on Head Runs of the Combinator *S*, and a Uniform Countdown](s10-clock-closure/main.pdf)
  **It proves, with a machine-checked proof, the exact head step at which a countdown term built from $S$ first fires, $22+(5m+13)g$ for every program of $m$ layers and register $g$, from a small algebra of clocks on head runs.**

  > A head step on a closed term built from the combinator $S$ pops the first three arguments $x,y,z$ of the head and pushes the arguments of $x$, then $z$ and $y\,z$. The step *fires* if $x$ is the normal term $F=S\,\mathsf{tw}_8\,\mathsf{tw}_8$, where $\mathsf{tw}_8$ is the right comb of eight $S$'s. A *clock* is a family of argument lists with a schedule: for each member, whether its head run fires and after how many steps. A member that does not fire must also never halt. We prove that clocks are closed under four operations on head runs (a quiet prefix followed by a clock, iteration with an exit at the bottom, sums and reindexing) and that an endless loop of quiet segments never fires and never halts. With these we prove that a countdown term with a program of $m$ layers, whose tags are arbitrary $S$-terms, and a register holding $g$ first fires at head step $22+(5m+13)g$, for every program and every $g$. Two capsules, of four and six leaves, first fire at steps $5g+1$ and $7g+1$. Every definition, lemma, theorem and corollary is mechanized in Lean 4, using only the axioms of propositional extensionality and quotient soundness. Every clock proved here fires for all parameters; the loop statement is proved but unused.
* [An *S*-Term Determined by Its Teeth Among Terms Without a Head Normal Form](s11-ct-no-exposed-move/main.pdf)
  **It proves, for one explicit term $p_K$ and every state of its run, that a term without a head normal form whose teeth are convertible with $p_K$'s is convertible with $p_K$, although no tooth of $p_K$ exposes the state that produced it. That converse of the tooth lemma is one of the two halves of the question whether a term's infinitary normal form determines its conversion class.**

  > The head run of a closed term built from the combinator $S$, if it never reaches a head normal form, splits off an infinite sequence of arguments, its teeth. Convertible terms of this kind have convertible teeth. We prove the converse for one term, $p_K$: every closed $S$-term without a head normal form whose $n$-th tooth is convertible with that of $p_K$, for every $n$, is convertible with $p_K$. The same holds, with the teeth shifted, for every state of $p_K$'s run. No tooth of $p_K$ exposes the state that produced it, so the proof cannot read the state from one tooth. Instead we show that a second run with the same teeth satisfies the spine equations of $p_K$, and that among the reducts of an explicit set of terms these equations have, up to convertibility, only the solutions given by $p_K$'s run. The proof is mechanized in Lean 4, and its only axioms are propositional extensionality and quotient soundness.
* [S-Terms with a Counter Run Are Determined by Their Teeth Among Terms Without a Head Normal Form](s12-ct-counter-grammar/main.pdf)
  **It extends the converse of the tooth lemma from one term to a family: every $S$-term whose run settles into an explicit grammar of counters with normalizing teeth is determined, up to conversion, by its teeth among terms without a head normal form, including the runs of $U_j\,U_m$, $X\,X$ and $X\,(S\,X\,c)$.**

  > The head run of a closed term built from the combinator $S$, if it never reaches a head normal form, splits off an infinite sequence of arguments, its teeth. Convertible terms of this kind have convertible teeth, and the converse, CT, is open. We prove CT against every term $p$ whose run is a counter run: from some point on its states are convertible with members of an explicit grammar of counters, and its teeth from then on normalize. Every term without a head normal form whose teeth are convertible with those of $p$ is then convertible with $p$. This covers the runs of $U_j\,U_m$, of $X\,X$, and of $X\,(S\,X\,c)$ for $c$ in an explicit set $\mathcal{K}$ of normal terms. The proof compares the two runs in normal form, where each state is a pair of normal terms. In the grammar, moves that copy the second term of the pair into the first recur. When a second run with the same teeth meets such a move, it reaches the same state at once or after finitely many further moves. The proof is not mechanized and case-splits on whether a term has a head normal form; finite facts about a 38-element model are checked by kernel evaluation in Lean 4.
* [Tree Equality Is a Congruence for the Combinator *S*](s13-tree-congruence/main.pdf)
  **It proves, with a machine-checked proof, that two $S$-terms with the same tree, that is, the same prefixes at every depth, keep the same tree in every context, and that the 38-element block model cannot tell them apart; so the conjecture that tree-equal terms are convertible is equivalent to its versions for contexts and for the tower of finite models of Prefix Regularity.**

  > Let $t$ be a closed term built from the combinator $S$. Head reduction gives $t$, for each depth $d$, a finite tree $\mathrm{NF}_d(t)$; these trees are the prefixes of its infinitary normal form. Call $t$ and $u$ tree-equal, $t\approx u$, if $\mathrm{NF}_d(t)=\mathrm{NF}_d(u)$ for every $d$. We prove that tree equality is a congruence: if $t\approx t'$ and $u\approx u'$, then $t\,u\approx t'\,u'$, so $\approx$ is preserved by every context. The proof mirrors each head step of $t\,u$ by head steps of $t'\,u'$, on the least relation that contains $\approx$ and is closed under application, and then compares prefixes by induction on the depth. With the same tools and a kernel-checked rank table, we prove that the 38-element block model, which decides head normal forms, is $\approx$-invariant: tree-equal terms have the same block. Four corollaries follow. The relation "tree-equal in every context" is $\approx$ itself. Equality of folds in every model of the tower of finite models that computes the prefixes is also $\approx$. Hence the conjecture that tree-equal terms are convertible is equivalent to its versions for these two relations. By an argument that uses Markov's principle, the conjecture is also equivalent to residual finiteness together with the $\approx$-invariance of the fold into every finite model of the $S$-law. The two theorems and their lemmas are mechanized in Lean 4, using only the axioms of propositional extensionality and quotient soundness. The corollaries, and the reading of $\approx$ as equality of infinitary normal forms, are not mechanized.

* [A Family of *S*-Terms Determined by Their Infinite Trees](s14-rigid-trees/main.pdf)
  **It proves that tree equality implies convertibility on the classes of the terms $G_i\,G_m$ ($G_i=e^i\,Q$, $e=S\,S$, $Q=S\,e\,e$, $m\ge1$), whose trees are infinite with a head normal form at every node of the rightmost path: the generator $G_i$ is rigid, since $Y\,G_m$ has the tree of $G_i\,G_m$ only if $Y=G_i$ as written.**

  > Every closed term built from the combinator $S$ has an infinitary normal form, its tree, and convertible terms have the same tree. Whether terms with the same tree are always convertible is open. Let $e=S\,S$, $Q=S\,e\,e$ and $G_i=e\,(e\,(\cdots(e\,Q)\cdots))$ with $i$ copies of $e$. We prove that if $Y\,G_m$ has the same tree as $G_i\,G_m$, where $i\ge0$ and $m\ge1$, then $Y$ is $G_i$ as written. The 38-element block model of the head-normal-form theorem confines $Y$ to three blocks; a finite computation on its table fixes the shape of the head normal form of $Y$; an induction on $i$ finishes the proof. As a corollary, every closed $S$-term with the same tree as $G_i\,G_m$ is convertible with $G_i\,G_m$. These terms do not normalize, and every node on the rightmost path of their trees has a head normal form. The proof rests on finitely many facts about the table of the block model and on the invariance of the block under tree equality, proved in a companion paper. The theorem and the corollary are mechanized in Lean 4, with the table facts checked by kernel evaluation, using only the axioms of propositional extensionality and quotient soundness. That the terms do not normalize is proved on paper only.

* [Convertibility of Tree-Equal *S*-Terms Splits into Spines and Turns](s15-spines-and-turns/main.pdf)
  **It proves that the conjecture that tree-equal $S$-terms are convertible (Lock 1) holds exactly when two other conjectures hold: CT, that the teeth of a term without a head normal form determine it up to convertibility, and NTD, that no descent through non-convertible pairs turns infinitely often. It also shows that Lock 1 on the class of one explicit term implies CT at an infinite family of explicit terms. All three conjectures remain open.**

  > Two closed terms built from the combinator $S$ are tree-equal when all finite prefixes of their infinitary normal forms agree, and Lock 1 is the conjecture that tree-equal terms are convertible. We split Lock 1 into two conjectures of different shape. A term without a head normal form has an infinite left spine, and along it an infinite sequence of arguments, its teeth. CT says that two such terms with pairwise convertible teeth are convertible. If two tree-equal terms are not convertible, some pair of their children is again tree-equal and not convertible, so the terms have an infinite descent through such pairs. NTD (no turning descent) says that no such descent turns off a left spine infinitely often. We prove that Lock 1 holds if and only if CT and NTD both hold. The forward direction is constructive; the converse uses excluded middle, for convertibility and for the existence of a non-convertible pair of teeth. We then prove three results about CT. CT at a term is equivalent to CT at the next term of its spine. When Lock 1 holds on the classes of the teeth, CT at a term is equivalent to Lock 1 on its class. Lock 1 on the class of a term passes to every node of its tree, so Lock 1 on the class of $D\,(c\,D)$, where $c=S\,(S\,S)$ and $D=S\,c\,c$, implies CT at an infinite family of explicit terms; the two smallest have sizes 50 and 56. A counterexample to CT at one of them would give an explicit pair of tree-equal terms that are not convertible. Of the 65,794 terms of sizes 8 to 13 without a head normal form, a program finds only 535 covered by the known cases of CT. Lock 1, CT and NTD remain open. Nothing in this paper is mechanized; the congruence theorem for tree equality that it uses is.

* [Convertible Teeth Determine *p<sub>K</sub>* over *S* but Not over *S*, *B*, *C*](s16-teeth-and-basis/main.pdf)
  **It shows that the theorem of S11, that the teeth of $p_K$ determine it among $S$-terms without a head normal form, depends on the basis: over $S$, $B$ and $C$ an explicit 188-atom term has the teeth of $p_K$ and is not convertible with it. A cancellation law for pure $S$ shows that five of the six passages of that term's run have no counterpart over $S$.**

  > The head run of a closed combinatory term without a head normal form splits off an infinite sequence of arguments, its teeth. For one term $p_K$ built from the combinator $S$ it is known that, among $S$-terms without a head normal form, every term whose teeth are convertible with those of $p_K$ is convertible with $p_K$. We show that this depends on the basis. Over $S$, $B$ and $C$ it fails: we give a closed term $q_{BC}$ of 188 atoms, built from $S$, $B$ and $C$, whose $n$-th tooth reduces to the $n$-th tooth of $p_K$ for every $n$, and no reduct of which is built from $S$ alone. So $q_{BC}$ is not convertible with $p_K$, over every basis between $\{S,B,C\}$ and $\{S,K,I,B,C\}$. The program contains no $I$ and no fixed-point combinator. In place of $I$ it uses $B\,C\,C$, which acts as the identity only when three arguments follow; in place of a fixed-point combinator it uses a family of terms that gains one such wrapper per round. We then compare with $S$ alone. A cancellation law holds there: if an $S$-term applied to fresh variables reduces to a term applied to the same variables, the variables can be removed from both sides. With the node-tracing lemma behind it, the lemma behind the known fact that $S$ has no fixed-point combinator, and the known fact that reduction never shortens an $S$-term, we show that five of the six passages of the run of $q_{BC}$ have no counterpart over $S$. We do not prove that every rival must use such passages. The result concerns $p_K$ only; whether teeth determine other terms, over $S$ or over larger bases, is not settled here. Nothing in this paper is mechanized.

* [The *SKI* Self-Interpreter, Mechanized](self-interpreter-mechanized/main.pdf)
  **It machine-checks a published self-interpreter for *SKI* against the terms as printed, so the 618-atom interpreter and 43-atom unquoter are certified to do what their paper claims, and it adds a converse of fuel adequacy, which shows that the interpreter's answer does not depend on its budget.**

  > A recent paper gives a self-interpreter for the $SKI$ combinator calculus: a 618-atom closed term `whnfF` that runs a Scott-encoded term to weak head normal form under a fuel budget, with a 43-atom unquoter `UQ`, and proves that the interpreter is adequate. We prove in Lean 4 that the definitions bracket-abstract to the printed terms, that these satisfy step and fuel adequacy as stated, and that `UQ` inverts the encoding. We also prove a converse of fuel adequacy and the independence of the answer from the budget, from which the paper's three listed consequences follow.
