\begin{abstract}
A closed term built from $S$ alone has a head normal form when it reduces to $S$,
$S\,X$ or $S\,X\,Y$. Barendregt, Endrullis, Klop and Waldmann asked whether a 35-state
tree automaton they gave accepts exactly these terms (\emph{Dance of the Starlings},
Question~10). We prove that it does. The automaton is a homomorphic image of a 38-element
model of the $S$-law, so its state is a reduction invariant, and rejection implies the
absence of a head normal form. For the converse we read a weight along the spine of a
term; a finite certificate of small integers makes the weight, with two tie-breakers,
decrease at every head step. The whole proof is mechanized in Lean~4 with no axioms beyond
propositional extensionality and quotient soundness, and the finite facts are checked by
kernel evaluation.
\end{abstract}
